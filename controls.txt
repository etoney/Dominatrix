===============================================================================
  DOMINATRIX  //  CONTROLS QUICK REFERENCE
  Castle Danger Music Company
===============================================================================

Dominatrix is a glue compressor for your master bus, with saturation, analog
character and a soft clipper that takes the place of your limiter. Put it LAST
on your master channel.

There are no threshold, ratio, attack or release knobs. DISCIPLINE sets all of
them for you, and they keep adjusting to the music. You can watch what she's
doing in THE RULES panel.

Tip: double-click a knob to reset it. Double-click the value under a knob to
type an exact number.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISCIPLINE  (the big knob)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  How hard Dominatrix holds the mix together. 0 = off (no compression at all).

  Around 20-40%   gentle glue, 0.5-2 dB of gain reduction. Most mixes live here.
  Around 50-70%   firm control, denser and more "finished".
  75-100%         heavy, pumping-free squash. Good for parallel use with MIX.

  What she sets for you:
    THRESHOLD  follows the level of your mix, so DISCIPLINE feels the same
               whether your mix is quiet or hot.
    RATIO      from 1.5:1 (gentle) up to 4:1 (full discipline).
    ATTACK     10-30 ms. Punchy material gets a slower attack so the
               transients (kick, snare) still hit; dense material gets a
               faster one.
    RELEASE    automatic, two-stage: short peaks let go fast, long sustained
               compression lets go slowly, so it glues without pumping.
    MAKEUP     automatic, so turning DISCIPLINE up doesn't just make it
               quieter.
    The low end (below about 90 Hz) is kept out of the detector so the kick
    and bass don't make the whole mix duck. Left and right are linked so the
    stereo image stays put.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SATURATION + SATURATION TYPE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SATURATION adds harmonics. 0 = clean. The level is compensated, so turning
  it up adds colour, not just volume. Pick the flavour:

  TAPE      Warm and smooth. Loud high frequencies get softened first, like
            real tape.
  TUBE      Rich even (2nd) harmonics. Sweet and thick.
  IRON      Output-transformer weight: the low end saturates more than the
            top, so bass gets fat and solid.
  CONSOLE   Subtle summing-bus glue. The gentlest one.
  WHIP      Bite and edge. Firm odd harmonics with a little upper-mid push.
            Use a little!

  Switching types crossfades smoothly, so you can flip between them while
  the music plays.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALOG
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Character and imperfections of older analog gear. 0 = digital clean.
  Turning it up adds, a little at a time:
    - tape wow & flutter (very slight pitch wobble)
    - a low-end "head bump" and a softer top end
    - left and right channels that don't quite match (level and tone)
    - a touch of crosstalk between left and right
    - a little component non-linearity
    - tape hiss and a faint mains hum, way down in the noise floor
    - slow "drift", as if the gear is warming up
  It's all subtle on purpose. 10-30% is plenty for most masters.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MIX
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Parallel ("New York") compression. 100 = all Dominatrix. Lower values blend
  the uncompressed, unsaturated mix back in. Dry and wet are kept perfectly
  in time, so there's no phasing.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WIDTH + STEREO  (new in 1.1)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WIDTH changes how wide the mix sounds. It splits the mix into the centre
  ("mid": kick, bass, lead vocal, snare) and the sides (stereo guitars, keys,
  reverb, room) and turns only the sides up or down. The centre stays put.

    100%       no change (default)
    above 100  wider and more open (up to 200%)
    below 100  narrower; 0% is fully mono (handy for checking your mix)

  80-140% is the useful range on a master. Past 150% (the knob's arc turns
  red) mixes start to sound phasey and hollow.

  Built-in safety:
    - The low end (below about 120 Hz) is never widened, so kick and bass
      stay solid.
    - Loudness is compensated, so wider doesn't just mean louder.
    - The ceiling and true-peak protection still apply after WIDTH.

  PHASE      shows how mono-safe your mix is. +1 = mono, around +0.3 to
             +0.8 = a healthy stereo mix, 0 = very wide, below 0 (red) =
             parts of your mix will cancel out on mono speakers.
  BASS MONO  makes everything below about 120 Hz completely mono, so the
             kick and bass sit dead centre on every system (and cut cleanly
             to vinyl). Most masters benefit from it.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT + CEILING  (this replaces your limiter)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CEILING sets the highest your master can ever go:

    -1 dB    LOUD MASTER. Use this for releases and streaming.
    -6 dB    HEADROOM. Use this when you're sending the mix to a mastering
             engineer, or want room for more processing later.
    OFF      No clipper. OUTPUT is just a volume knob.

  OUTPUT pushes the mix into a soft, transparent clipper sitting at the
  ceiling. The more you turn OUTPUT up, the more it clips, and the louder the
  master gets, but it never goes past the ceiling. Below the clipper's knee
  (about 2 dB under the ceiling) nothing is touched at all.

  The ceiling is a TRUE-PEAK ceiling: a final safety stage catches the tiny
  in-between-sample peaks that clipping creates, so a -1 dB ceiling really
  is -1 dBTP, which is what the streaming services ask for.

  Watch the CLIP meter: 1-3 dB is usually invisible, 4-6 dB gets loud and
  dense, beyond that you'll start to hear it on cymbals and vocals.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Oversampling for the saturation and the clipper: 2x, 4x (default) or 8x.
  Higher = cleaner top end and more CPU. 4x is plenty while you work; pick
  8x for the final bounce if you like. Changing it changes the plugin's
  latency (your DAW compensates automatically).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MATCH  (level-matched listening)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Louder always sounds better, which makes it hard to judge what you're
  doing. MATCH turns the output down (or up) to the same loudness as the
  input, so when you bypass and un-bypass you hear what Dominatrix does, not
  just "louder". A yellow MATCHED LISTENING bar shows on the meters while it's
  on. TURN IT OFF BEFORE YOU BOUNCE. It isn't saved in presets.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METERS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  GAIN REDUCTION  the big needle: how much DISCIPLINE is compressing (dB).
  THE RULES       what the automatic compressor has set right now.
  IN / OUT        peak levels in and out.
  CLIP            how hard the clipper is working (dB). The white line holds
                  the recent peak. Shows OFF when the ceiling is off.
  LUFS            loudness of the output (before MATCH). SHORT-TERM is the last
                  3 seconds; INTEGRATED is the whole song since you last reset
                  it. Click the LUFS display to reset INTEGRATED. As a guide,
                  most streaming services play back around -14 LUFS.
  The red lamp next to the logo burns brighter the harder she works.
